Research

Burrowing robots.

Soft robotic systems at the Faboratory, Princeton University, advised by Prof. Rebecca Kramer-Bottiglio.

Journal · Frontiers in Robotics and AI · 2025

Grand Challenges for Burrowing Soft Robots

This review explores the role of soft materials in biological burrowing and their implications for robotic design. We analyze gaps between natural burrowers and soft robotic burrowers, identify grand challenges, and propose opportunities to enhance burrowing performance.

Conference · IEEE RoboSoft · 2026

Common Ground: Standardizing Granular Media Characteristics for Burrowing Robots

We propose standardized metrics to characterize granular media testbeds. Understanding how soil properties affect soil state — and thus the required burrowing forces — can help identify and evaluate suitable burrowing methods for specific granular environments.

Variable Stiffness and Variable Size Particles for Reconfigurable Granular Metamaterials

We introduce variable stiffness and variable size particles to create reconfigurable granular metamaterials with tunable mechanical properties — enabling adaptive structures that can be programmed on the fly.

Field work

NASA Glenn Research Center.

Two residencies as part of my NASA Space Technology Graduate Research Opportunities fellowship.

🚀 NASA NSTGRO Award 80NSSC24K1339

Resource Ingesting Soft Robotic Skins for In Situ Regolith Sampling

Funded by a NASA Space Technology Graduate Research Opportunities Award (2024), my research investigates soft robotic mechanisms for burrowing into and collecting planetary regolith — combining soft robotics with geotechnical principles for space exploration applications.

As part of this fellowship, I completed two Visiting Technologist Experience residencies at NASA Glenn Research Center:

  • May – July 2025 · TREC Lab: Investigated the effect of intruder softness on intrusion forces in GRC-3 lunar regolith simulant.
  • May – June 2026 · Excavation Lab: Characterized testbed resetting consistency and studied intruder softness effects in GRC-3b simulant.
